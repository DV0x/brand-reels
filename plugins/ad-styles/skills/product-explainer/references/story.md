# Story: a film, not an animated slideshow

**The failure to avoid:** one picture per voice line, a cut on every line, things that pop in and then sit there.
That's an animated slideshow, and it reads as a lab report even when the copy is good. Fun comes from a situation,
a hero object, timing and motion, never from puns.

## The spine
Before any shot, write one sentence: **the hero** (one object, usually the viewer's own thing or the product), **what
happens to it**, **the turn**, and **the payoff**. For example: "The coffee jar sits in the fridge among the onions and soaks
up their smell, until it moves to a dark cupboard and a fresh pouch arrives."

## Rules
1. **One hero object** that the camera follows through one change. The viewer always knows where to look.
2. **Two or three places,** each revisited and changed (the fridge, then the cupboard, then the fridge again with the
   onion alone). One continuous world, travelled by the camera, beats separate scenes.
3. **Five hard cuts or fewer.** The camera travels between ideas: push in, pan, tilt, follow the object. Cutting into
   the end card counts as one.
4. **Something new every 1.5 to 2 s:** an action, an arrival, a change of state. A slow zoom is not an event.
   The QA flags gaps over 2.2 s.
5. **Anything held stays alive:** steam, a breathing light, a swaying plant, a flickering flame, a ticking clock.
6. **Hands do the actions:** pick up, point, turn, pour, place.
7. **The photo shows what the product is; the drawing shows what a camera can't:** the inside, time passing, where it
   comes from, a smell, a number as a thing.
8. **Labels, arrows and circles act:** they draw on, pop with a small settle, point at the thing, then leave. Nothing
   just appears and sits.
9. **Re-hook every 6 to 10 s** with a turn: a new question, a reversal, a reveal.
10. **Statements live in the scene** where they can: on a tag, a stamp, a sign, a calendar, a sticky note. Big type
    on a clean area otherwise, inside the safe box.
11. **The end card** (2 to 3 s): the true product, big; the brand name; one line, often the action. It's the one place
    the product is the whole picture.
12. **Sound follows the picture:** every action gets its sound, on the frame it happens.

## The shot list (write it before the code, keep it in the project as SHOTS.md)
For each shot:
- its name and its lines (and so its time)
- what's in frame: places, hero, props, depth layers
- the camera: where it starts, where it goes, cut or travel in
- the actions, timed to words: "on 'smell', the onion's smell drifts into the jar"
- the sound for each action
- what carries over into the next shot

Count the events per shot. If a shot has fewer than one per 2 s, add actions, not decoration.

## Contact sheet
The sheet shows one frame per shot (at its key moment) with its voice and on-screen words. It's for checking the look,
the layout and the story's progress at a glance. Motion and timing are judged on the video.

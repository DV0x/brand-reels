# Benchmarks: elite hooks and brand films

The bar for every film. Each entry is real, and its numbers come from the source linked at the end (checked
2026-10-08). "Brand's own figure" marks a number from an award entry or the brand itself: a claim, not an audit.

## How to use this file
- Read it before you write hooks (SKILL.md, step 2), and again before the treatment.
- Pick 1 or 2 entries that fit the topic. Write what to learn from each and what not to take: in `research.md` for the
  hooks, in `TREATMENT.md` for the film.
- Learn the grammar only: how the first second works, how the question is held, how the proof is shown. Never reuse an
  entry's words, subject, product, character or joke. Your hook comes from this brand's own fact.
- These are the bar, not templates. If your hooks would look weak next to them, write more hooks.
- Which entry shows which technique: the technique table in [copy.md](copy.md) ("Techniques").

## Short-form reels

**1. The Whole Truth, "Three desserts" (Instagram, 2026).** 49.3M plays; the account has 421K followers.
- First second: a dessert moves toward the lens.
- Voice: "Three desserts. Same calories. My body should have treated them the same. It didn't." Rehook: "One barely
  moved the needle."
- Grammar: the viewer's belief, then a 2-word turn; which dessert stays hidden past 10 s.

**2. The Whole Truth, "Date powder is just sugar?" (Instagram, 2026).** 5.7M plays (13 times its followers).
- First second: dates being ground to powder.
- On screen and voice: "Date powder is just sugar?"
- Grammar: a sceptic's question about the brand's own ingredient, held as the open loop.

**3. FoodPharmer, the sugar board (Instagram, 2023).** 27.0M plays; 4.0M followers.
- First second: real cans on a whiteboard, with the sugar sachets under them uncovered.
- On screen: "COKE HAS 8 TEASPOONS OF SUGAR".
- Grammar: a famous named product plus a number made physical; the proof is in the first second.

**4. FoodPharmer, Bournvita (India, April 2023).** About 12M views (reported). The maker sent a legal notice, the child
rights commission acted, and about eight months later the drink's added sugar was cut by 14.4%.
- Hook: he reads a well-known children's health drink's own label on camera. As reported, his claim was that it is
  almost half sugar (his exact words are not checked).
- Grammar: a trusted household product, with its own label as the proof.

**5. Kallaway, "Uber Eats for sunlight" (Instagram, 2024).** 11.1M plays (24 times his 460K followers).
- First second: a hand holds a globe, then satellite footage.
- On screen: "UBER EATS FOR SUNLIGHT". Voice: "This company lets you order sunlight on demand." Rehook at 8 s: "But
  that's not even the craziest part."
- Grammar: the text and the voice say different things that add up.

**6. Kallaway, the Vegas Sphere (Instagram, 2024).** 8.0M plays.
- Voice: "The tech in the Vegas Sphere... Biggest screen ever built, 20 times bigger than an IMAX. But get this, the
  screen is the least impressive part."
- Grammar: the subject in the first words, a number for scale, a short turn, a rehook that promises more.

**7. Stanley, the car fire (TikTok, November 2023).** The customer's video: 60M to 94M views (outlets differ). The
company president's reply: 32M+ views. Stanley bought her a new car.
- First second: a burned-out car; the cup still in its holder. She shakes it, and the ice rattles.
- Grammar: proof you can see and hear. It started as a customer's video; the brand's fast reply made the story its own.

**8. Dove, "Reverse Selfie" (2021).** Cannes Lions 2022 Gold (Print) and more; cross-category sales +11.9% (brand's own
figure).
- First second: a teenager's perfect, edited selfie on a phone screen. The edits then undo, one by one, back to her
  real photo.
- Grammar: a hidden process played backward, on the phone screen itself.

## Brand films

**9. Dollar Shave Club, "Our Blades Are F***ing Great" (YouTube, March 2012).** About 12,000 customers in the first 48
hours; 4.75M views in three months; 27M+ by 2020.
- First second: the founder walks toward the camera, deadpan.
- Grammar: he asks the viewer's own doubts out loud and answers them; the price ($1 a month) is the surprise fact.

**10. Squatty Potty, "This Unicorn Changed the Way I Poop" (YouTube and Facebook, October 2015).** Online sales +600%
(company figure); 40M+ Facebook views by late November 2015.
- The film: a prince and a puppet unicorn that poops rainbow soft-serve explain that a normal toilet "kinks" the colon,
  and that raising the feet into a squat unkinks it.
- Grammar: an awkward body fact made watchable; the product is the answer to the fact.

**11. Poo-Pourri, "Girls Don't Poop" (2013).** 6M views in the first week, 26M+ later; YouTube's fifth most-viewed ad of
2013; $4M of backorders in a week (brand's own figures).
- Grammar: the taboo said plainly in the title; the product removes the embarrassment.

**12. Blendtec, "Will It Blend?" (from 2006).** The series passed 65M YouTube views by 2009; the company reports sales up
700% (series-wide, brand's own figure). The iPhone episode was the most popular.
- Grammar: a question in the title, a famous object, and the demo as the answer.

**13. Volvo Trucks, "The Epic Split" (November 2013).** 73M+ YouTube views by mid-2014; Cannes Lions Grand Prix in Film
and Cyber, 2014 (for the Live Test series).
- The film: a calm close-up of Jean-Claude Van Damme. The camera pulls back: he stands across two reversing trucks and
  does the split.
- Grammar: one impossible picture proves one product fact (precise steering).

**14. Burger King, "Moldy Whopper" (February 2020, 45 s).** Cannes Lions Grand Prix (Outdoor), 2021; 8.4B impressions and
sales +14% (brand's own figures).
- The film: a Whopper is built and labelled "Day 1"; a time-lapse over 34 days shows it rot; the end line is "The beauty
  of no artificial preservatives."
- Grammar: the hidden fact shown over time; the ugly picture is the proof. Note: viewers' interest dropped when the
  decay began (Ace Metrix). It won through press and talk, not through watch-through.

**15. McDonald's Canada, "Behind the scenes at a McDonald's photo shoot" (2012).** About 6 to 6.7M views at the time.
- The film: a customer's question (why does the burger look different in the ads?). The marketing director buys one in
  a restaurant and takes it to the photo studio, where a stylist builds the ad burger over hours.
- Grammar: the customer's own doubt as the hook; the answer is the process, shown.

**16. Dove, "Evolution" (October 2006, 75 s).** Two Cannes Lions Grand Prix (Film and Cyber), the first work to win in
two categories; 12M views in the first year.
- The film: an ordinary woman sits down; a time-lapse of make-up and hair, the photo shoot, the photo edit (a longer
  neck, bigger eyes), the billboard. End text: "No wonder our perception of beauty is distorted."
- Grammar: a hidden process shown fast, from start to end; one line names the fact.

**17. Purple, "How to Use a Raw Egg to Determine if Your Mattress is Awful" (2016).** Company revenue went from $65.5M
(2016) to $196.9M (2017) (filings, which do not credit the video); 194M+ views (agency's own figure).
- The test: four raw eggs under a 330-pound glass pane, dropped about 3 feet onto mattresses. On other mattresses all
  four break; on Purple they survive.
- Grammar: a test the viewer can picture at home; the result is the product fact (soft and firm at once).

**18. Domino's, "Pizza Turnaround" (December 2009).** Same-store sales +14.3% in Q1 2010, a record for the category at the
time.
- The film: it opens on a real tweet, "Dominos tastes like cardboard". Staff read the complaints; the company admits the
  old pizza was bad and remakes it.
- Grammar: the brand reads its own worst review first; honesty is the hook.

**19. CeraVe, "Michael CeraVe" (January and February 2024).** Cannes Lions 2024 Grand Prix (Social & Influencer); 32B
impressions; sales +25% (brand's own figures).
- The hook: for three weeks, 450+ creators spread a rumour that the actor Michael Cera made CeraVe. The Super Bowl ad
  answered it: dermatologists made it.
- Grammar: a question held open for weeks; the answer is the brand's true fact.

## Three whole films, beat by beat
In our own words; no scripts are copied.
1. **Moldy Whopper (45 s):** the burger is built (0 to 4 s) → "Day 1" → the time-lapse of 34 days of decay → the line.
2. **Dove Evolution (75 s):** an ordinary face → the make-up time-lapse → the photo shoot → the photo edit → the
   billboard → two girls look up at it → the line.
3. **McDonald's photo shoot:** the customer's question → a burger bought in a restaurant → the studio → the stylist
   builds the ad burger → the photo and the edit → both burgers side by side.
